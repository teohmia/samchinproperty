Buying & Renting in Singapore

A plain-English walkthrough of how property transactions actually work here — residential and commercial, buying and renting — plus the stamp duties involved.

Please note: this page is general information, not legal or financial advice — Sam is not a lawyer or financial advisor. Stamp duty rates and rules are set by IRAS and do change over time; the figures below reflect published rates as of September 2026. Always confirm current rates on iras.gov.sg, and check the specifics of your situation with Sam or your conveyancing lawyer before making a decision.

Buying Residential Property

The typical path for a private resale purchase, from viewing to keys.

  1. View and negotiate. Once you and the seller agree on a price, the seller's agent prepares an Option to Purchase (OTP).
  2. Pay the option fee. Usually around 1% of the purchase price, paid to secure the OTP. This grants you the exclusive right to buy within the option period (commonly 2–3 weeks).
  3. Arrange financing. Get an Approval-in-Principle (AIP) from a bank, or confirm your cash position, before the option period runs out.
  4. Exercise the OTP. Pay a further amount (often around 4%, bringing the total deposit to roughly 5%) to exercise the option and form a binding contract.
  5. Engage a conveyancing lawyer. They handle the title search, contract, and eventually the completion.
  6. Pay Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD), and Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) if it applies to you, within 14 days of exercising the OTP. See rates below.
  7. Complete the purchase. For a resale property this is typically 8–10 weeks after exercising the option, when the balance price is paid and keys are handed over.

Renting Residential Property

What happens between finding a place and getting the keys.

  1. View the unit and agree on the rent and key terms (lease length, furnishing, start date) with the landlord.
  2. Submit a Letter of Intent (LOI) with a good-faith deposit — usually one month's rent — to show you're serious. This is refundable if the landlord doesn't proceed, but typically forfeited if you withdraw without cause.
  3. Sign the Tenancy Agreement (TA). This sets out the lease term, rent, deposit, and responsibilities of both parties.
  4. Pay stamp duty on the lease within 14 days of signing (30 days if signed overseas). This is usually the tenant's responsibility. See the formula below.
  5. Pay the security deposit — typically one month's rent per year of lease for terms of two years or less (e.g. two months for a two-year lease).
  6. Move in with an inventory list signed by both parties, so the unit's condition is documented from day one.

Commercial Property

Buying or leasing office, retail or warehouse space works a little differently from residential.

Buying commercial property

The OTP-and-exercise process is broadly similar to residential purchases, with a few key differences: ABSD does not apply to commercial and industrial property — only BSD. If the seller is GST-registered, GST may apply on top of the purchase price, so it's worth checking this early. It's also worth confirming the property's permitted use under URA zoning before committing.

Leasing commercial space

Commercial leases usually start with an Agreement for Lease (or Letter of Offer) covering rent, lease term and fit-out period, followed by the formal Tenancy Agreement. Compared to residential rentals, expect: longer lease terms (often 2–3 years with a renewal option), a larger security deposit (commonly 3 months plus GST), and rent quoted per square foot rather than as a flat monthly figure. Stamp duty on a commercial lease is calculated the same way as for residential leases (see below).

Lease (Tenancy) Duty — residential & commercial

For leases of 4 years or less: 0.4% of the first year's rent, plus 0.2% for each additional year, based on the higher of the contractual or market rent.

WORKED EXAMPLE A 2-year lease at $2,500/month ($30,000/year):
Year 1: 0.4% × $30,000 = $120
Year 2: 0.2% × $30,000 = $60
Total stamp duty: $180

Leases with an average annual rent of $1,000 or less are exempt. Duty must be paid within 14 days of signing (30 days if signed overseas) — late payment incurs a penalty of up to 10% of the duty owed, plus interest.
